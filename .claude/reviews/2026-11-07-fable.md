# ntm review, 2026-11-07

## 1. Where things stand

The 0.3 cleanup did real work: the plugin reads as one person's design again,
the words are consistent (no ticket/groom/voice/ADHD anywhere in plugin text),
`notify` and the messenger have real contracts, and the big slop generators
(fixed job kinds, weekly-planning, the Amazon steps database, timezone math) are
gone. What's left is structural, not cosmetic: the new-user flow is long and
ends in the one step nobody has tested (the `.plugin` accept card), the
regenerate-and-reinstall loop is the system's core friction and every small
settings change goes through it, and three things that should be one-page
contracts (the style skill's procedure, the bulk-planning job, reviewing) live
in the wrong file. One fact to flag for the "use it for a few days" plan: the
ntm copy installed on this machine still exposes `ntm:completed`,
`ntm:weekly-planning` and `ntm:amazon-alerts`, so it predates #31, #26 and #30
even though both it and main say 0.3.2. He's not running main.

## 2. What's strong

- `notify`, `ready-check`, `configure-notifications`, the three reminder skills
  and the messenger: tight, one job each, readable by a non-programmer, failure
  lines a job can act on.
- The rules/values/calls split (`conventions` / `ntm-user:settings` / backend
  guide) is the right domain cut and it's stated once, at the top of
  `conventions`.
- CONTRACT.md plus linear.md is a genuine backend contract. The "two fatal gaps,
  the rest survivable" framing is exactly typical-flow-first.
- `configure-schedule`'s "approve a table before anything is created" and "match
  jobs by the skill their prompt loads, never by title" are the right calls.
- DECISIONS.md as a record of what's in force (not a dated log) is working; I
  could reconstruct intent from it alone.

## 3. The most important problems

**1. The flow ends in the untested step, and every settings change repeats it.**
`settings` §4-6 (lines 108-128): build in `/tmp/ntm-user`, zip, copy to
`/mnt/user-data/outputs/`, hope for an accept card ("untested", per DECISIONS).
A label rename or a timezone fix means regenerate, download, reinstall, new
chat. For a real user that's the moment ntm feels like software instead of a
helper. _0.4 design input_ (question 1 below). For now: _fix in the
simplification pass_ by making the packaging step idempotent and explicit about
what to do when no card appears, since that's the path he'll hit first.

**2. The most-run generated artifact has a four-bullet spec in someone else's
skill, and the spec has a hole.** `ntm-user:style` runs on every reminder; its
procedure is authored fresh by the user's agent from `settings` lines 86-106.
Meanwhile `messenger.md:125` says "the style counts and records ids from the
tone", `builder.md:101-103` says "the procedure counts them from the message
history", and the spec in `settings` never says to record ids. That's the "tasks
that keep slipping never get a harder reminder" item in DECISIONS, and it's a
one-line spec gap, not a storage design problem. _Fix in the simplification
pass_: add "record the ids the tone names with each history entry" to `settings`
§3. _0.4 design input_: the style contract needs its own home.

**3. Bulk planning is a 30-line job prompt the plugin can't reach.**
`configure-schedule:104-134` duplicates the reminder skills' job-run block
(ready check, messenger handoff, fact-line format, final reply rules, agent
fallback) into a user-owned prompt. It contradicts "a job is a three-line prompt
that only loads a skill" (DECISIONS) and `configure-schedule:84-85` ("a plugin
update reaches the jobs without touching them"), which is why a "Re-syncing a
prompt" section had to exist. Removing `weekly-planning` for its name moved the
logic to the worst place. _His decision_: either a thin skill that owns the
chain (name it with him), or `plan --auto <range> --notify` that asks
`show-completed` and the messenger itself, the way `show-completed --notify`
already does.

**4. `configure-schedule` over-asks a new user.** Walking the schedule (lines
136-163): per job days/time/title, then planning diffs, then model, then per-job
app tokens, then the jitter formula (letters in the title mod 29 plus 1) and a
Pacific-time window that's Claude-infra trivia; then per job a permission-mode
sentence and a connector check. For three jobs that's a dozen rounds. _Fix in
the simplification pass_: defaults for all of it, one approval table, one
sentence after creation. Jitter stays as "not on :00 or :30"; the formula and
the Pacific note go. App tokens only on a re-run when they ask.

**5. `configure-todo-list` asks and creates more than a fresh list needs.** The
price watch project question (`linear.md:192-197`) assumes they know what the
watch is; Statuses (lines 93-101) runs a call and shows a list with nothing to
decide; Planning asks estimate type (`linear.md:199-206`), estimate pattern, and
"anything else" of someone who has never estimated; Labels creates both Amazon
labels and Databases creates four documents whether or not anything will use
them. `pause`, the estimator and `price-watch` already create their databases on
first write; only Message history needs setup, for its `Keep:` line. _Fix in the
simplification pass_: price watch and Amazon only when they ask for it; statuses
silent unless something's off; one estimate question with a default; databases
created by their owners (DECISIONS already lists this as a 0.4 input; it's a
cut, not a design).

**6. The atypical backend path sits in the middle of the typical one.**
`configure-todo-list:67-91` ("Your own") is 25 lines of contract-walking for a
backend nobody has described yet, between The backend and Statuses. A Linear
user reads it every time. _Fix in the simplification pass_: move it to
`references/own-backend.md`, leave one sentence.

**7. Reviewing lives in an agent file, and the review skill is a `find`
command.** `estimator.md:95-258` is a chat procedure (it says so: "it runs in
the chat"), and `review-todo-list` (31 lines) tells the agent to locate the file
with a glob. A user who opens `review-todo-list` to understand reviewing learns
nothing; the estimator is two-thirds skill. DECISIONS parks this for 0.4, but
it's a cut-and-paste with no design in it. _His decision_ on timing; I'd do it
in the simplification pass.

**8. `conventions` carries prose the jobs don't need, and every run loads it.**
337 lines plus the 265-line Linear guide plus settings, on every job. "What this
todo list is" (36-63) is the README's job; "Creating tasks when the user asks"
(323-331) is chat-only; Parent task/Sub-task are still in Vocabulary and
`parent id` in the standard fields after the "purge parents" decision (32 parent
mentions across 9 files). _Fix in the simplification pass_: cut the mission
prose, move chat-only sections out, finish the purge or reverse the decision.

**9. Three homes for "install, new chat, run self-test" and a confusing line
inside setup.** `configure:89-92`, `configure-todo-list:248-252` and
`settings:127-128` all say it. And `conventions:24-25` makes the agent say "not
configured; run /ntm:configure" twice while the user is inside `/ntm:configure`
(loaded by `configure-todo-list` and again by `configure-schedule`). Small slop
in the same family: `today:40-45` states the `(started)` rule twice; README
gives the settings location three times (24-28, 136-140, 200-206); CONNECTORS.md
is the README's Connectors and Requirements sections again. _Fix in the
simplification pass_: one home each; `conventions` stays quiet when a caller
brings its own values.

**10. PR #33 deletes the names record and replaces it with a test that covers a
quarter of it.** The bats test holds skills, agents, `name` and `displayName`,
and it's clean, readable shell. But deleting `.claude/NAMES.md` drops the
approval record for settings keys, label names, database titles, style profile
headings, skill arguments, and the 0.4-only names, none of which the test
checks. The PR body's "Left for the repo owner" section describes a
`tests/expected/README.md`, header comments and a guard hook that aren't on the
branch (I checked: `tests/expected/` holds only the three contract files).
That's the description-drifts-from-diff pattern he just moved away from. _His
decision_: merge the test, keep the record (trim NAMES.md to the untested
sections, or fold them into DECISIONS), and have the author fix the body.

## 4. The new-user flow, traced

What an agent on a fresh install does for `/ntm:configure`, with the Linear
path.

**Opening.** Loads `ntm-user:settings`, fails, says two intro lines. Fine. About
you is filled from the session and confirmed in one round. Good; this is the
model for the rest.

**Todo list.** Loads `conventions` (says "not configured; run /ntm:configure" to
a user who is running it). Asks the backend. Reads `linear.md`. If they have no
Linear: three steps outside chat, wait for "done". Then the connector check: if
Linear isn't attached, `Configure-todo-list FAILED` and "come back", which in
practice means a new chat and the wizard from the top. **Stall 1**, and the
likeliest one: the README's step 1 says connect Linear first, but nothing in
chat checks before the wizard starts. Direction: `configure` does the ToolSearch
in its opening and says "connect Linear on the Connectors tab, then start a new
chat and run this again" before anything else.

Then: team (fine), Parent auto-close suggestion, price watch project question
(**over-ask** for most), statuses listed with nothing to decide (**carry
text**), label table plus five creates, four database reads plus three or four
creates, estimate type, estimate pattern, "anything else about planning"
(**over-ask**: three estimate-shaped questions), and the sections shown back.
About ten rounds for a part that needs three (team, labels ok?, how do you size
things?).

**Notifications.** Channel, keys, test push, wait for "got it", one question.
Four rounds, all earning their place. Good.

**Style.** "Just remind me" means plain, but Plain (`configure-style:59-75`)
still walks Register, What gets you to read, levels, Motifs, Examples.
**Over-ask**: the builder already has a complete plain example
(`builder.md:156-198`); plain should be "here's the plain profile, change
anything?" in one round. A theme is rightly longer.

**Schedule.** Loads `conventions` again ("not configured" again). Then the dozen
rounds from problem 4. The jobs are created now, before `ntm-user` exists, so if
the user stops here, three jobs start firing failure pushes. **Break-ish**:
direction is create them off and have `self-test` turn them on as its last step,
or create them after install. His call; it changes the one-chat promise.

**Packaging.** `settings` §1-6: author `plugin.json`, two skills (the style
skill's procedure from four bullets), zip, copy to outputs, accept card.
**Untested step** at the end of a forty-minute chat; `/tmp` and the outputs path
are assumptions about the host. Then "install, open a new chat, run self-test",
and if self-test fails on the backend they're back in `configure-todo-list`,
then packaging, then reinstall.

**Text the agent carries and doesn't need in this flow:** `configure:76-82` ("in
case they ask"), `configure-todo-list:67-91` (own backend), the Statuses
section, `configure-schedule:155-160` (jitter formula, Pacific window), the
per-job "Say how it runs" and "Check its connectors" sections.

**Suggested shape for the first session:** keep the five parts and the order;
aim for About you 1 round, todo list 3, notifications 4, style 1 (plain) or 6
(theme), schedule 2 (table, approve). Everything else becomes a default or a
re-run question.

## 5. For the 0.4 talks

**Q1. Where do the user's values live, and what forces a regenerate?** Today
everything is in the generated plugin, so every change reinstalls. DECISIONS
holds the alternative as "an option, not decided": settings in a document on the
backend. _Recommendation:_ secrets stay in the user plugin (Pushover keys,
nothing else needs hiding); settings and the style profile live in backend
documents found by title, like the databases already are. The user plugin then
regenerates only when the backend or channel changes, which is almost never, and
`/ntm:settings` becomes "edit this document". This is the single decision that
most reduces friction, and it fits "a person can read and own it": a Linear doc
is more his than a zip.

**Q2. What is a contract, and how many are there?** The decided list is
`todo-list`, `ready-check`, `self-test`, `notify`, with `settings` and `style`
floating. _Recommendation:_ a contract is something the base calls by a fixed
name whose implementation varies per user. That's three: `todo-list`
(operations, already CONTRACT.md), `notify` (channel), `style` (the messenger's
call). `ready-check` and `self-test` are the same procedure for everyone and run
_against_ the contracts; they belong in the base. Each contract gets one
reference page in the base (`references/contracts/<name>.md`) that says the call
shape and return lines; the user plugin's skill is its implementation. That
fixes problem 2's missing home.

**Q3. Does the user plugin bundle the backend connector?** Decided yes, with
"which Linear wins" open. _Recommendation:_ reverse it. 0.3 already finds tools
by name and the Connectors tab works; bundling creates a second Linear, an OAuth
flow, and a plugin that can't be regenerated without re-authenticating. The open
question disappears if the decision does. Flag that this undoes a decided item.

**Q4. What does `update` do, and how many entry points does the user plugin
carry?** "Every command starts in the user plugin" taken literally means a
forwarder per skill, regenerated on every base release. _Recommendation:_ the
user plugin carries `setup`, `update`, `help` and the three contracts; jobs and
the user call base skills by name. `update` is `configure` with a diff: read
what's installed, say what the new base needs that's missing or renamed, ask
only that, regenerate, and re-sync job prompts (which stays trivial only if
prompts stay three lines, which is problem 3 again). The alias plugin is a
vanity feature; park it past 0.4.
