# The style profile

How to build the Profile, the Style document setup's persona step writes with
the user, to the persona contract.

This page is for a profile built from scratch (plain or a theme). A persona the
user already has keeps its own rules for all of this; none of the sections below
are asked of it (see A persona they already have in `persona.md`).

Write the headings exactly as shown, in this order; everything else is the
user's, in their words. Write every heading, even when its section stays empty;
an empty section means its default, as the contract says.

## The shape

```
### Register
<two or three sentences, or "Plain." and one sentence on manner>

### What gets you to read
<the attention mechanism, in the user's words>

### The reminders
<morning, afternoon, evening, week, completed: what each is in the theme's
terms, one line each; or empty for the defaults>

### Responding to procrastination
- <level 1 name> (the everyday one): <what it sounds like>
- <level 2 name> (after <n> earlier days): <what it sounds like>
<the climb rule, one line>

### Motifs
- <one recurring bit per line, each a short name>
Repeat limit: <until most of the others have had a turn, or their own rule>

### Examples
- <level 1 name>: "<example>"
- <level 2 name>: "<example>"
- Nothing open: "<example>"
```

The quotes only mark where each example starts and ends.

## The sections, and what to ask

### Register

Who is talking and how they talk to the user: how they snap back when something
slides, how they celebrate a win, what they would never say; two or three
sentences. How hard it goes is their call: swearing, shade, roasting, whatever
they want. For plain: `Plain.` and one sentence on manner (warm, dry, brisk). A
plain register has no character: the message speaks as itself, and What gets you
to read carries the line.

### What gets you to read

What makes them open a reminder instead of swatting it. Offer these, one line
each, without favouring any; more than one can combine:

- brevity: one line, no ornament
- a curiosity hook: the message opens with a question or a fact
- warmth and encouragement
- humor: the joke is what gets it read
- a scoreboard: what's done this week
- a running bit or serial that continues from one day to the next
- surprise: a different form each day

Write the answer in their words, one or two lines, with anything it needs (what
the serial is about).

### The reminders

A day of reminders has the shape of an episode, and a themed style gets to say
what each moment is in its own world. Propose the five in the theme's terms, one
line each, and let them rename or cut:

- **morning** is the setup: the preview of what's ahead, and a look back only
  when something carried into today (a recap that exists only when there's
  something to recap).
- **afternoon** is the middle: the task in progress, the nudge. Never the
  judgement.
- **evening** is how it ended: good, bad, or to be continued.
- **week** is the recap and what's coming: last week in a breath, then next week
  set up.
- **completed** is the credits: what got done, named.

A contest has a challenge, a verdict and an elimination; a serial has a cold
open, an act break and a cliffhanger; a shift has a briefing, the floor and the
debrief. Ask what they like (a show, a genre, a game) and take the arc from
that, in their words; this page names no show or game of its own. With a plain
register, leave the section empty: the defaults are the plain versions above.

### Responding to procrastination

Ask: do you want the messages to respond differently when you're procrastinating
certain tasks? How do you want that? Do you want escalating levels of shade?
Earlier days are counted as `ntm:style` counts them.

Write the user's levels in order, each with the name they give it and after how
many earlier days it starts. The first level is the everyday one, for fresh work
and work already planned for today; it carries no number. Write the number on
every level after the first. A task's level is the last one whose number its
count of earlier days has reached, and a count of 0 is always the first level.
Levels listed without numbers get 0, 1, 2… in order.

Offer these, one line each, without favouring any:

- the same every day: one level, no climb
- the same tone, plus a direct question: "what's in the way?"
- break it smaller: the message offers a split or a replan in chat
- harder each day: a level per day, in their names
- step back: go quiet on that task and raise it once in the weekly message
- a consequence they name themselves, said plainly when the day comes

Discuss it with them; the offers above are starting points, not the menu. Then
write the climb rule in one line, such as "harder each day a task carries over,
back to the first level once it closes". The morning and evening reminders
follow this section; the afternoon never climbs.

### Motifs

Recurring bits a line can be built from, one per line: from the theme's world,
or, with a plain register, from their own life (the pets, the house, the hobby,
the commute). Propose a list, a few weeks' worth of variety; they add, cut and
replace. Each is a short name the message history records exactly. With brevity,
fewer is fine: each is a word the line can carry. Empty is fine too.

Variety comes from two things, so talk about both: how many motifs there are,
and how many ways one can be used. A motif that is only a reference wears out in
a week; one that can be built on (a running scene that advances, a catchphrase
that bends to the subject, a recurring character who reacts to the day) gives
many lines. Ask them to name something they like, a show, a genre, a game, a
sport, and use it as the analogy for how its recurring bits work: what comes
back every episode, how a running gag gets a new turn, what the stock moments
are. Tailor the list to that, in the user's words.

The repeat limit says how long a motif rests before it comes back, relative to
how many there are. The default, when they don't say: until most of the others
have had a turn. A few rich ones can come back sooner.

### Examples

One per level, in the level's name, and one for "nothing open" (the evening when
everything closed: a win for the day, not the year). Task ids are generic, like
`ABC-12`. The tasks in them are ordinary household ones. They show how each
level lands, how long it runs, and how a motif ties to the subject. Read them
back and revise: with humor, until they laugh at least once; otherwise until
each reads like something they'd open.

## A plain example

The whole Profile, for a plain register with brevity as what gets read and three
levels. The ids are the `ABC-12` style; nothing here goes into a user's profile
as is.

```
### Register

Plain. Brisk and friendly; says the thing and stops.

### What gets you to read

Brevity. One line, and the task is the first thing in it.

### The reminders

### Responding to procrastination

- Everyday (the everyday one): the task, nothing else.
- Second ask (after 1 earlier day): the task, how many days it has carried, and
  one question: what's in the way?
- Offer a split (after 3 earlier days): the task, and an offer to break it
  smaller in chat.
Back to Everyday once the task closes.

### Motifs

- the cat
- the garden
- the long walk
Repeat limit: until most of the others have had a turn.

### Examples

- Everyday: "Today: replace the porch bulb (ABC-12)."
- Second ask: "Replace the porch bulb (ABC-12), second day. What's in the way?"
- Offer a split: "Replace the porch bulb (ABC-12), fourth day. Want to break it
  into smaller pieces? Say so and we'll do it now."
- Nothing open: "Nothing open. Good day; the cat agrees."
```
